# Hawaii

What the world is, as the owner chose it in November 2026. It replaced the invented island in version 0.0.31. Milestone 4 is planned from this file. Distances are nautical miles unless they say otherwise.

## The idea

The real Hawaiian Islands, the way Rage does Zion: real ground from survey data, real satellite photographs laid over it, water and sky as in Rage. No landmarks built by hand and no invented places: what's there is what's in the data.

The owner's words: the highest resolution that will fit in the app, and the same everywhere. No coarse ground with sharp patches at the famous places.

## The islands

The eight main islands: Niihau, Kauai, Oahu, Molokai, Lanai, Maui, Kahoolawe and the Big Island. The small islets near them (Lehua, Molokini, Mokolii and the like) come along because they are in the data. The Northwestern Islands do not.

**Real size, real shape, real heights.** Nothing on an island is stretched or moved. Mauna Kea is 13,803 ft, Mauna Loa 13,679, Haleakala 10,023, Waialeale 5,148 and Kaala 4,025. The Na Pali coast and the Molokai sea cliffs are 3,000 to 4,000 ft, and Waimea Canyon is about 3,000 ft deep. Stretching the heights is one number in the script, for the owner to ask for after he has flown it.

**The channels are shortened.** At 500 knots it takes about 15 minutes just to cross the real 116 km (63 nm) from Kauai to Oahu. The rule: **every channel wider than 15 km (8 nm, about one minute at 500 knots), coast to coast, is cut to 15 km.** Narrower channels stay as they are: inside Maui Nui (Molokai, Lanai, Maui and Kahoolawe) they are already 9 to 15 km.

| Channel | Real | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| Kaulakahi, Niihau to Kauai | 27 km | 15 km |
| Kaieiewaho, Kauai to Oahu | 116 km | 15 km |
| Kaiwi, Oahu to Molokai | 42 km | 15 km |
| Inside Maui Nui (Pailolo, Auau, Kalohi, Alalakeiki) | 9 to 15 km | real |
| Alenuihaha, Maui to the Big Island | 48 km | 15 km |

So the islands sit in five groups (Niihau, Kauai, Oahu, Maui Nui and the Big Island). Each group keeps its own shape and its own north, and slides along the chain toward Oahu until its channel is 15 km wide. Oahu stays where it is. End to end, Niihau to Cape Kumukahi, the chain comes to roughly 450 km: about half an hour at 500 knots, against about 40 minutes for the real one.

## The ground and the pictures

| What | Source | How sharp |
|---|---|---|
| The ground's shape | USGS 3D Elevation Program, 1/3 arc-second (public domain) | a height every 10 m |
| The pictures | ESA WorldCover 2021: a cloud-free year of Copernicus Sentinel-2 photographs (CC BY 4.0) | a picture point every 10 m |
| Where the water is | ESA WorldCover 2021 land cover, and the sea | every 10 m |
| The runways | OurAirports (public domain) | their real ends, widths and headings |

Ten metres is the sharpest the free data gets for every island alike. A runway 150 ft wide is four or five picture points across, so the runways are drawn as well (below).

The photographs carry the satellite's own light and shadow, so the game's sun is gentle on them, and a light grain is added up close, as in Rage.

## The water

The sea is Rage's water: deep blue, paler toward the horizon where it reflects the sky, with the sun's glint on it and a faint ripple. No waves breaking, no reefs drawn under it. Lakes, reservoirs and fishponds are water too.

As before, **any touch of water ends the flight,** unless the crash override is on.

Beyond the data the sea goes on for ever.

## The sky

Rage's sky, which Wings already has: a dome fading to a pale horizon, a mid-afternoon sun, fair-weather cumulus painted on the dome, and haze that thickens from 16 km (9 nm) out and hides everything by 80 km (43 nm): the next island across a channel is clear, and from the middle of Oahu Molokai and Kauai show through it. Always a standard day: no wind, weather or turbulence.

Every runway is dead flat (0%), on the real ground as much as can be (the owner's choice, 17 Nov 2026: real slopes don't matter, a smooth roll does, and fewer edits mean fewer mistakes). Each runway's height is the middle value of the real ground under it, which moves the least ground, set to a height the tiles hold exactly so the saved runway has no steps; runways that cross, or whose flat ground meets, share one height. The ground is flat under the paint and 28 m past its edges and ends (so the drawn ground lies flat right to the paint's edge), and from there a bank slopes down or up to the real ground, which beyond it is left as it is: the levelling's change fades by 1 m for every 3 m, like a road embankment. Where the real ground itself falls away, the two add up, so a bank beside a gully is steeper than 1 in 3 for a short way: nothing natural is reshaped (the owner's choice). Under a runway, and wherever a bank raises the ground, is dry land. Taxiways, ramps and buildings are only in the photographs. All dry land is as firm as a runway, as before.

## North

The HUD's heading tape reads magnetic, as the charts and the runway numbers do. One variation for all the islands: **9.6° east**, the HNL VORTAC's. On 8R at Honolulu, pointed 090 true, the tape reads 080.

## The start

**Every flight starts on the numbers of runway 8R at Honolulu, on the Hickam side:** the 12,000 ft reef runway, the one the trade winds put in use, pointed east toward Diamond Head. Gear and flaps down, brakes on. The Hawaii Air National Guard flew F-4C Phantoms from Hickam from 1976 to 1987.

## The download

All of Hawaii is about **175 MB** (the owner agreed to as much as 300), kept on the phone so it flies with no signal. It downloads once, on Wi-Fi, from the start screen, which shows how far it has got ("Hawaii: 60 of 175 MB"). Fly waits until it is all there. If the download stops, it carries on from where it was next time.

The islands live in their own store on the phone, apart from the app. An update to the app doesn't download them again; only a new version of the islands does.

## How it's made

`terrain/make-hawaii.py` downloads the sources, cuts out the islands, moves the groups, levels the runways and writes the tiles to `terrain/hawaii/`. It runs on a computer, never on the phone. The app reads the tiles for the picture, and the flight model reads the same tiles for the ground, so what is seen is what is hit. Far away, the picture draws the ground from coarser copies of the map on coarser grids, each with enough cells that a summit is never more than a few pixels short of its real height at any distance, so peaks don't grow as you close in. `tests/hawaii.test.js` checks the summits, the channels, the runways and the sea against this page.
