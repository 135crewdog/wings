# Changelog

What changed in each version a player can see, newest first.

## 0.0.23

- The castle school: the owner's own, on its shelf above the loch's south shore. A cluster of dark stone towers with steep pointed roofs, a great hall, a greenhouse and a boathouse; the tallest spire stands 720 feet above the ground. It is hidden: it can't be seen from the lowlands.
- With crashes on, the castle is solid. With the crash override on, the jet passes through it.

## 0.0.22

- The viaduct: the owner's own, a curved stone railway viaduct 2,100 feet long and 400 feet high, on eleven tall arches, across the head of the canyon with the river under it. Every arch is big enough to fly through.
- With crashes on, the viaduct is solid: touch a pier or the deck and the flight ends. With the crash override on, the jet passes through it.

## 0.0.21

- A new app icon: the owner's F-4C, banking against a blue sky.

## 0.0.20

- A new island, to the owner's own map. Mountains in an arc across the north round the loch; a canyon with a river from the loch to the south-west coast; a desert plateau with mesas in the west, running up into the mountains; a volcano in the south with a crater and a glowing lava lake; the fields and the air base in the east; a round cove a mile across on the east coast.
- New paint for the new country: red and tan rock in bands, black lava, wide sand on the desert's shore, white chalk round the cove.
- Lava spills out of the crater through a breach in its west rim and runs down the volcano's west side, fanning into three glowing streams to the sea, its crust riding downhill.
- The redwoods: a forest of giants about 300 feet tall, south and east of the volcano. Solid at the treetops, like the woods. Woods and redwoods are now painted as trees: a field of crowns on top, trunks at the edges.
- Water you can judge your height over: patches of swell and small flecks on the sea, the loch and the river, the sun's glitter toward the sun, and the sky's colour in it at a low angle.
- With crashes on, the river and the lava (the lake and the streams) end a flight at any touch, like the sea and the loch.
- Still to come: tree crowns that stand up in three dimensions near the jet, and every landmark (castle, viaduct, the Strip, the Door).

## 0.0.19

- Woods: blocks of woodland 40 feet tall, in the farmland and across the western lowlands. They are solid: with crashes on, flying into one ends the flight, and the radar altitude reads to the treetops. None stand on the airfield, off the runway's ends, or in the glens.
- Trees along the tracks between the fields. These are scenery: you can fly through them.

## 0.0.18

- With crashes on (the crash override off): flying into a mountainside ends the flight, and so does any touch of the sea or the loch, however gentle. A landing in a field still survives.
- With the crash override on, nothing changes: nothing ends a flight but the pilot, and water holds the jet up.

## 0.0.17

- The island. The flat test field is gone: flights start on runway 28 of the air base, in the farmland in the island's east, with the highlands to the north, the sea all round and the south coast's cliffs.
- The land is painted to match the jet: four-sided fields, moor on the hills, rock on the heights, chalk cliffs, sand at the shore.
- The radar altitude now differs from the altitude: the runway is 30 feet above the sea, and the hills come up under you.

## 0.0.16

- A sun and clouds in the sky. The clouds are fair-weather cumulus, far off and still; you can't fly through them. As in Rage, painted to match the jet.

## 0.0.15

- Look around: drag a finger across the view and the camera swings around the jet. Let go and it comes back behind. As in Rage.

## 0.0.14

- The F-4C rotates when it should. With the stick full back the nose wheel lifts at 150 knots, where it was past 190, and the jet lifts off at about 174. Flaps up, it lifts off at 211 where it was 267.
- The stick is a little lighter at low speed: less back tilt holds level flight in the pattern.

## 0.0.13

- A new F-4C: the owner's own, built in Blender, in Southeast Asia camouflage. It replaces the stand-in, and the stand-in's credit is gone from the screen.
- Its main wheels sit farther behind the centre of gravity, as the real jet's do, so the nose comes up later on takeoff: about 195 knots with the stick full back.

## 0.0.12

- Easier on the battery. The screen is kept awake only in flight, so a phone put down on the pause or start screen can sleep. And the app stops drawing when nothing is moving.

## 0.0.11

- Crash override: a switch on the pause screen. It starts on, and with it on nothing ends a flight: a hard landing bounces, and so does flying into the ground. Turn it off and a hard arrival ends the flight, as before.
- With crashes counted, a wing-low landing is no longer punished harder than a level one.

## 0.0.10

- Running the engine up against the brakes no longer tips the picture. The nose strut was far too soft: the nose dipped 2° and the horizon jumped. It now dips half a degree.
- The jet stands on the runway, level, as the model is built. Its tyres used to sit 6 inches into the pavement.

## 0.0.9

- The rudder is steadier. In a roll it moves smoothly and no longer slams from stop to stop, and after a roll the nose settles in one or two swings where it used to wobble for several seconds. A hard roll at low speed still slips the nose: that is the airplane.

## 0.0.8

- The control surfaces look right. Each travels as far as the real F-4's: stabilator 21° up and 9° down, flaps 60°, ailerons 30° down, spoilers 45° up, rudder 30° each way.
- The stabilator moves less the faster you go, so at high speed it no longer hangs in the air like a barn door.
- Nothing changes in how the jet flies.

## 0.0.7

- Readouts: airspeed in knots and altitude in feet across the top. Below 2,000 feet above the ground, radar altitude shows under the altitude.
- Near the stall the picture shakes like buffet, harder the closer you get.

## 0.0.6

- First flight. Tap Fly, and the F-4 flies: tilt the phone for the stick, slide the throttle on the left edge, and tap Gear, Flaps and Brakes on the right.
- A pause button, with Resume, Restart and Recenter. The flight pauses by itself when the app is left or the phone is turned.
- A hard crash ends the flight; Restart puts the jet back on the runway.

## 0.0.5

- The F-4 sits on a runway, seen from behind: the test field for first flight. The look-around view, with the parts moving by themselves, is gone.

## 0.0.4

- The F-4 rolls the way the real one does: an aileron goes down on one wing and a spoiler comes up on the other. The ailerons no longer move up.

## 0.0.3

- An F-4 hangs in the sky. Drag to look around it; its gear, flaps and controls move by themselves. It is a stand-in model by Artbor, credited beside the version.

## 0.0.2

- The rotate screen is black to the edges of the screen, with no blue bands at the top and bottom.

## 0.0.1

- The empty app: a sky, with the name and version in the corner. It installs on the home screen, works offline and offers its own updates.
