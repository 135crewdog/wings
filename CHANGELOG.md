# Changelog

What changed in each version a player can see, newest first.

## 0.0.46

- The haze is pushed back: it starts 9 nm out instead of 2 and hides everything only by 43 nm instead of 13, so the next island across a channel is in plain view. The sky just above the horizon is paler, the colour of the haze, so the sea and the sky meet with no line.

## 0.0.45

- A smoother frame rate high up: above about 5,000 ft the finest ground detail, too small to see from there, is left out.

## 0.0.44

- A smoother frame rate: the ground behind and beside you, and beyond what the camera can reach, is no longer drawn. Nothing on screen changes.

## 0.0.43

- Peaks and ridges are their real height from as far away as you can see them: they no longer grow as you close in. The far ground is drawn on finer grids, so the phone works harder on the ground's shape.

## 0.0.42

- The speedbrakes pitch the nose down about half as much as in 0.0.35 to 0.0.41, as gently as before their drag was doubled, and still slow the jet as hard.

## 0.0.41

- Every runway is dead flat: no more bump on the takeoff roll at about 125 knots, and the runway paint lies right on the ground. Each runway sits at the height of the real ground under it that moves the least earth, with a bank like a road embankment where it's raised or dug in. The first launch after the update downloads about 64 MB of new airfield ground.

## 0.0.40

- A Settings button on the start screen opens the pause screen (the map and the switches) before you fly; there, the top button says Fly.
- Fewer credits: only the photographs' credit stays, in small print along the foot of the map. The version shows on the start screen only.

## 0.0.39

- The pause screen's map no longer comes back squashed after turning the phone to portrait and back.

## 0.0.38

- Tap the map on the pause screen to zoom in round the jet, about 80 km across, from sharper photographs; tap again to see the whole chain. Every pause opens on the whole chain.

## 0.0.37

- A map on the pause screen: the menu moves to the right, and the left shows the islands as the photographs you fly over, north up, with a magenta dot on every airport and a green F-4C where the jet is, pointing the way it's heading.

## 0.0.36

- A developer overlay: a Developer switch on the pause screen, off to start with, shows a small box of raw numbers in the top left (frame rate, the phone's tilt, the stick and throttle, airspeed, g, angle of attack and sideslip, altitude, where the jet is and what ground is loaded round it), for troubleshooting and screen recordings.

## 0.0.35

- The boards bite twice as hard. At idle through 400 knots the jet now slows about 10 knots a second with them out (6 before), 400 to 250 knots in about 22 seconds (35 before), and the nose wants to drop a little more as they open.

## 0.0.34

- With the HUD switched off, the white airspeed and altitude across the top are gone: only the HUD's own airspeed and altitude stay, green, in their places, radar altitude and all.

## 0.0.33

- The HNL VORTAC on the HUD, as Rage's steerpoint: a diamond on the station when it's in front of you, and when it isn't, a small circle above the flight path marker with a tail pointing the way to turn. Its distance, in nautical miles, sits lower right: "12.4 > HNL".

## 0.0.32

- The HUD's heading tape is cleaner, as War Wagon's: the heading sits in the tape's own row under the pointer, and the tape's numbers slide past beneath it as you turn, so the top of the screen is shorter.
- It reads magnetic, as the charts and the runway numbers do (Hawaii's variation is 9.6° east): on 8R at Honolulu it reads 080.

## 0.0.31

- Hawaii. The invented island is gone; the world is the real Hawaiian Islands, all eight, from survey heights and satellite photographs a point every 10 metres. Every island is its real size and shape and height, Mauna Kea 13,800 ft and all; only the channels between them are cut to 15 km, about a minute at 500 knots.
- Every flight starts on the numbers of runway 8R at Honolulu, the reef runway, pointed east at Diamond Head. Every paved runway of 3,000 ft or more on the islands is drawn with its numbers and can be landed on: 26 runways at 19 airfields.
- Rage's water and sky: a deep blue sea with the sun's glint on it, soft cumulus, haze toward the horizon.
- The first time, the start screen downloads Hawaii, about 175 MB, and says how far it has got. Then it stays on the phone, for flying with no signal; an app update never downloads it again.

## 0.0.30

- Afterburner plumes, one behind each engine, after Rage's but made to look like the F-4's J79s from photographs of Phantoms climbing out in burner: the nozzles glow orange-yellow inside, with a short yellow-white core and a salmon-orange flame behind each. They grow with how much afterburner the engines give, about one and a half metres at the least and four and a half at full, and show the engine, not the throttle: they light a moment after the throttle goes into the burner range and are gone at MIL.

## 0.0.29

- A HUD, Rage's: thin green symbols over the view. Heading across the top, a pitch ladder that rolls with the view, the flight path marker and the waterline, airspeed on the left and altitude on the right (radar altitude with an R below 2,000 ft), and g, angle of attack, Mach and the engine's power in the lower left.
- A HUD switch on the pause screen, beside crash override. It starts on; off, the plain airspeed and altitude come back. The choice is kept.

## 0.0.28

- Speedbrakes. A fourth button, Boards, opens the two panels under the wings in a second and a half; tap again to close them. They are real drag, acting where the panels are: at idle through 400 knots the jet slows about twice as fast with them out, and the nose drops a touch as they open.

## 0.0.27

- The owner's F-4C, revised: the windscreen is rounded at the front, as on the real jet, with other small fixes to its looks. It flies exactly as before; its wheels and every point that can touch the ground are where they were.
- The app icon shows the revised jet.

## 0.0.26

- The Strip begins: a mile and a half of road running north and south across the desert west of the canyon, with nineteen plain hotels along it, 180 to 505 feet tall. Empty lots are left for the famous places, which arrive one at a time.
- With crashes on, the hotels are solid. With the crash override on, the jet passes through them.
- The jet's wheels no longer sink into the runway: the runway's paint was being drawn over the bottoms of the tyres.

## 0.0.25

- Durdle Door: the owner's own, a limestone arch 330 feet high standing in the sea off a headland on the east coast, just south of the cove. The hole through it is 230 feet wide and 200 tall: fly through it along the coast, north or south.
- With crashes on, the rock is solid. With the crash override on, the jet passes through it.

## 0.0.24

- The land no longer jumps as you fly over it. Far ground is drawn coarser than near ground, and it used to switch from one to the other in a single frame; now it eases across.
- Trees no longer pop into being. They are drawn half a mile farther out, and each one grows out of the ground as you come to it.

## 0.0.23

- The castle school: the owner's own, on a crag 330 feet above the loch's south shore. A cluster of dark stone towers with steep pointed roofs, a great hall, a greenhouse and a boathouse; the tallest spire stands 720 feet above the ground. It is hidden: it can't be seen from the lowlands.
- With crashes on, the castle is solid. With the crash override on, the jet passes through it.

## 0.0.22

- The viaduct: the owner's own, a curved stone railway viaduct 2,100 feet long and 400 feet high, on eleven tall arches, across the head of the canyon with the river under it. Every arch is big enough to fly through.
- With crashes on, the viaduct is solid: touch a pier or the deck and the flight ends. With the crash override on, the jet passes through it.

## 0.0.21

- A new app icon: the owner's F-4C, banking against a blue sky.

## 0.0.20

- A new island, to the owner's own map. Mountains in an arc across the north round the loch; a canyon with a river from the loch to the south-west coast; a desert plateau with mesas in the west, running up into the mountains; a volcano in the south with a crater and a glowing lava lake; the fields and the air base in the east; a round cove a mile across on the east coast.
- New paint for the new country: red and tan rock in bands, black lava, wide sand on the desert's shore, white chalk round the cove.
- Lava spills out of the crater through a breach in its west rim and runs down the volcano's west side, fanning into three glowing streams to the sea, its crust riding downhill.
- The redwoods: a forest of giants about 300 feet tall, south and east of the volcano. Solid at the treetops, like the woods. Woods and redwoods are now painted as trees: a field of crowns on top, trunks at the edges.
- Water you can judge your height over: patches of swell and small flecks on the sea, the loch and the river, the sun's glitter toward the sun, and the sky's colour in it at a low angle.
- With crashes on, the river and the lava (the lake and the streams) end a flight at any touch, like the sea and the loch.
- Still to come: tree crowns that stand up in three dimensions near the jet, and every landmark (castle, viaduct, the Strip, the Door).

## 0.0.19

- Woods: blocks of woodland 40 feet tall, in the farmland and across the western lowlands. They are solid: with crashes on, flying into one ends the flight, and the radar altitude reads to the treetops. None stand on the airfield, off the runway's ends, or in the glens.
- Trees along the tracks between the fields. These are scenery: you can fly through them.

## 0.0.18

- With crashes on (the crash override off): flying into a mountainside ends the flight, and so does any touch of the sea or the loch, however gentle. A landing in a field still survives.
- With the crash override on, nothing changes: nothing ends a flight but the pilot, and water holds the jet up.

## 0.0.17

- The island. The flat test field is gone: flights start on runway 28 of the air base, in the farmland in the island's east, with the highlands to the north, the sea all round and the south coast's cliffs.
- The land is painted to match the jet: four-sided fields, moor on the hills, rock on the heights, chalk cliffs, sand at the shore.
- The radar altitude now differs from the altitude: the runway is 30 feet above the sea, and the hills come up under you.

## 0.0.16

- A sun and clouds in the sky. The clouds are fair-weather cumulus, far off and still; you can't fly through them. As in Rage, painted to match the jet.

## 0.0.15

- Look around: drag a finger across the view and the camera swings around the jet. Let go and it comes back behind. As in Rage.

## 0.0.14

- The F-4C rotates when it should. With the stick full back the nose wheel lifts at 150 knots, where it was past 190, and the jet lifts off at about 174. Flaps up, it lifts off at 211 where it was 267.
- The stick is a little lighter at low speed: less back tilt holds level flight in the pattern.

## 0.0.13

- A new F-4C: the owner's own, built in Blender, in Southeast Asia camouflage. It replaces the stand-in, and the stand-in's credit is gone from the screen.
- Its main wheels sit farther behind the centre of gravity, as the real jet's do, so the nose comes up later on takeoff: about 195 knots with the stick full back.

## 0.0.12

- Easier on the battery. The screen is kept awake only in flight, so a phone put down on the pause or start screen can sleep. And the app stops drawing when nothing is moving.

## 0.0.11

- Crash override: a switch on the pause screen. It starts on, and with it on nothing ends a flight: a hard landing bounces, and so does flying into the ground. Turn it off and a hard arrival ends the flight, as before.
- With crashes counted, a wing-low landing is no longer punished harder than a level one.

## 0.0.10

- Running the engine up against the brakes no longer tips the picture. The nose strut was far too soft: the nose dipped 2° and the horizon jumped. It now dips half a degree.
- The jet stands on the runway, level, as the model is built. Its tyres used to sit 6 inches into the pavement.

## 0.0.9

- The rudder is steadier. In a roll it moves smoothly and no longer slams from stop to stop, and after a roll the nose settles in one or two swings where it used to wobble for several seconds. A hard roll at low speed still slips the nose: that is the airplane.

## 0.0.8

- The control surfaces look right. Each travels as far as the real F-4's: stabilator 21° up and 9° down, flaps 60°, ailerons 30° down, spoilers 45° up, rudder 30° each way.
- The stabilator moves less the faster you go, so at high speed it no longer hangs in the air like a barn door.
- Nothing changes in how the jet flies.

## 0.0.7

- Readouts: airspeed in knots and altitude in feet across the top. Below 2,000 feet above the ground, radar altitude shows under the altitude.
- Near the stall the picture shakes like buffet, harder the closer you get.

## 0.0.6

- First flight. Tap Fly, and the F-4 flies: tilt the phone for the stick, slide the throttle on the left edge, and tap Gear, Flaps and Brakes on the right.
- A pause button, with Resume, Restart and Recenter. The flight pauses by itself when the app is left or the phone is turned.
- A hard crash ends the flight; Restart puts the jet back on the runway.

## 0.0.5

- The F-4 sits on a runway, seen from behind: the test field for first flight. The look-around view, with the parts moving by themselves, is gone.

## 0.0.4

- The F-4 rolls the way the real one does: an aileron goes down on one wing and a spoiler comes up on the other. The ailerons no longer move up.

## 0.0.3

- An F-4 hangs in the sky. Drag to look around it; its gear, flaps and controls move by themselves. It is a stand-in model by Artbor, credited beside the version.

## 0.0.2

- The rotate screen is black to the edges of the screen, with no blue bands at the top and bottom.

## 0.0.1

- The empty app: a sky, with the name and version in the corner. It installs on the home screen, works offline and offers its own updates.
